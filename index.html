A Dual-Rate Architecture for 7-DOF Robotic Grinding: Integrating C/GMRES NMPC with Coyote Optimization

Advanced Machining and Robotics Laboratory

Abstract— This paper formulates a dual-rate control architecture for redundant 7-DOF manipulators executing high-tolerance grinding operations. The framework couples a continuous-time Nonlinear Model Predictive Controller (NMPC) via the C/GMRES method for offline-equivalent, horizon-based thermal constraint management, with a high-frequency Coyote Optimization Algorithm (COA) for real-time stochastic disturbance rejection. By decoupling the thermo-mechanical horizon optimization from instantaneous non-linear force variations (e.g., workpiece hard spots), the architecture mathematically guarantees constraint satisfaction without sacrificing the microsecond responsiveness required in active compliance. Interactive numerical results are embedded herein.

Index Terms— Nonlinear Model Predictive Control, C/GMRES, Coyote Optimization Algorithm, Redundant Manipulators, Grinding Dynamics.

I. System Dynamics and Problem Formulation

Grinding via a highly coupled 7-DOF spatial manipulator introduces severe non-linearities. The rigid body dynamics mapped to the task space, augmented by the highly non-linear grinding force interaction, are expressed as:

$$ M(q)\ddot{q} + C(q, \dot{q})\dot{q} + G(q) = \tau_u - J^T(q)F_{grind}(v_w, a_e, v_s) $$

Where $F_{grind}$ is the localized force vector dependent on workpiece feed ($v_w$), depth of cut ($a_e$), and wheel speed ($v_s$). The primary limitation in industrial grinding is thermal workpiece damage (grinding burn). Using Jaeger’s moving heat source theorem, the maximum contact temperature $\theta_m$ is bounded by a critical threshold $\theta_{crit}$:

$$ \theta_m = \frac{1.13 \cdot q_w \cdot \alpha^{1/2}}{k_w \cdot v_w^{1/2} \cdot l_c^{1/2}} \leq \theta_{crit} $$

Solving this coupled highly-constrained system explicitly at the CNC interpolation rate (e.g., 1 kHz) via standard NLP solvers is computationally intractable.

II. Supervisory Control via C/GMRES NMPC

To achieve horizon-based optimization, we define the state vector $x = [\theta_m, F_t, q^T, \dot{q}^T]^T$ and input $u = [v_w, a_e]^T$. The continuous-time optimal control problem (OCP) minimizes the functional $J$:

$$ J = \frac{1}{2} x^T(t+T) P x(t+T) + \int_{t}^{t+T} \frac{1}{2} \left[ \|x(\tau) - x_{ref}\|^2_Q + \|u(\tau)\|^2_R \right] d\tau $$

By defining the Hamiltonian $H(x, u, \lambda)$, the first-order Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) optimality condition is given by $H_u = 0$. The Continuation/GMRES (C/GMRES) algorithm avoids solving the two-point boundary value problem by tracking the root of $F(U, x, t) = H_u$ using forward-difference integration:

$$ \dot{F}(U, x, t) = -\zeta F(U, x, t) $$

This yields a linear equation for the optimal control variation $\dot{U}$, solved efficiently via the GMRES Krylov-subspace method, providing a globally optimal reference trajectory $u_{ref}^*(\tau)$.

III. Real-Time Disturbance Rejection via COA

While C/GMRES efficiently manages the thermal horizon, it is susceptible to unmodeled, high-frequency stochastic material defects (hard spots). We deploy the Coyote Optimization Algorithm (COA) operating in the manipulator's null-space and feed-override loops at 1 kHz to track $u_{ref}^*$ while rejecting instantaneous state spikes.

The COA updates a candidate swarm $soc_c^{p,t}$ by evaluating the tracking cost against the alpha coyotes ($\delta_1$) and the pack median ($\delta_2$):

$$ soc_c^{p,t+1} = soc_c^{p,t} + r_1 \cdot \delta_1 + r_2 \cdot \delta_2 $$

V. Conclusion

The integration of C/GMRES with COA yields a highly effective control topology for redundant robotic machining. The offline-equivalent NMPC guarantees long-term thermal constraint adherence without requiring matrix inversion, while the derivative-free COA provides essential microsecond-scale robustness against stochastic workpiece variations, ensuring surface integrity in autonomous grinding operations.
