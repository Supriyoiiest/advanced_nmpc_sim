Hybrid Control Architecture Overview

This application interactively visualizes a state-of-the-art dual-layer control architecture for industrial grinding operations. Grinding is a highly non-linear process requiring strict constraints to prevent workpiece thermal damage (burn) while maximizing wheel life and material removal.

⮧

Offline Supervisory Level

Utilizes Non-linear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) via CasADi and C/GMRES solver. It generates the baseline optimal trajectory (X_ref, U_ref) by predicting future plant states, ensuring all thermal and actuator constraints are respected over the entire machining pass.

⮢

Online Real-Time Level

Employs the Coyote Optimization Algorithm (COA) to ingest the offline trajectory and act as an ultra-fast tracking controller. It reacts to real-time state estimates (via EKF and DAQ sensors) to reject immediate disturbances at the high-frequency CNC interpolation rate.

Phase 2: Offline NMPC (CasADi + C/GMRES)

Non-linear Model Predictive Control predicts future states over a time horizon. By tuning the cost function matrices (Q for states, R for control effort), we shape the optimal trajectory. Adjust the sliders to see how the NMPC solver balances aggressive machining against the temperature constraint limits.

State Penalty (Q matrix)

Higher Q forces the system to reach the optimal machining target (higher removal rate) quickly, keeping temperature strictly below the burn limit constraint.

Control Penalty (R matrix)

Higher R penalizes large, sudden changes in CNC actuators. This results in smoother, slower control effort but takes longer to reach target efficiency.
